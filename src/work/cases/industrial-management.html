Industrial Downtime Management

Mobile-first PWA for logging, handling, and analyzing industrial line stoppages. It covers the full cycle — from the QR code stuck on the machine to the indicator on the monthly report — with photo, video, and audio capture directly in the browser, voice dictation, and QR scanning without leaving the app.

  • Offline-first PWA
  • QR code via camera
  • In-browser audio + voice
  • Framework-free SPA
  • Modular architecture

Overview

A simple problem, solved in depth.

When a machine stops, the team has to respond fast: what stopped, when, why, who's handling it, and how much time it cost? Paper doesn't survive the shift. Spreadsheets become islands. The indicator arrives too late.

This system centralizes the entire flow in an interface that works in the technician's hands on the factory floor — with offline logging, media captured on the spot, and synchronization when the signal comes back.

How it works

From the factory-floor entry to the monthly indicator.

  1. 1

    Badge ID

    The technician types their badge number. The system looks up the record and auto-fills name, role, and manager — avoiding redundant typing on the floor.

  2. 2

    Department → Machine cascade

    Choosing the department filters available machines. On save, there's cross-validation: if the machine doesn't belong to the selected department, the record is blocked with a clear message.

  3. 3

    Structured classification

    Failure type, root cause, and component come from controlled lists — with a <datalist> of suggestions. No free-text fields turning into report noise.

  4. 4

    Component action + preventive

    Clickable chips categorize what was done on the spot and what remains as a preventive action. It becomes structured data for the Pareto chart, not loose text.

  5. 5

    Attachments at the time of the event

    Camera photo, short video (20 MB limit), and audio up to 60 s — all captured in the browser itself using MediaRecorder and uploaded with a progress bar.

  6. 6

    Closure and status

    Status (already closed or in progress) plus total time in free format (45min, 1h30, 2h). The notes field accepts voice dictation via SpeechRecognition.

Technical decisions worth noting

Six points in the system that show product maturity, not just UI.

PWA

Installable and offline-aware

The app has manifest.webmanifest and sw.js. Asset caching, its own theme, and launch from the phone's home screen — no app store, no download, no friction for the operator.

UX

Badge becomes identity, not typing

With 10 digits, the system looks up the technician in state.db.tecnicos and auto-fills name, role, and manager. If the badge matches nobody, it warns. If it matches the currentUser, it falls back to the current user — without blocking the field workflow.

Integrity

Cross-validation on save

The cascade filters in the select, but the save step re-validates: if the machine doesn't belong to the chosen department, the record is blocked with an explicit message. No inconsistent data entering the database because of stale state in memory.

In-browser media

Photo, video, and audio without a dedicated backend

Camera via capture="environment", video capped at 20 MB, and audio recorded with MediaRecorder — 60 s max, with a live counter and preview. Upload runs in parallel with a counter "uploading 2 of 5…".

Voice

Voice dictation in the notes field

The notes field has a microphone button that uses SpeechRecognition. In an industrial environment, a technician wearing gloves appreciates it: they describe the event by talking, not typing.

Architecture

Swappable persistence adapter

All reads/writes go through data/api.js. Today it persists to localStorage — tomorrow it becomes Supabase or any other back-end without touching a service or a view. Zero coupling with storage.

What stayed with us

A system that operations actually uses isn't the one with the most features — it's the one that survives the shift. A good chunk of the time was spent listening to the maintenance team to figure out what was essential in the middle of a stoppage and cutting the rest. That's how features like voice dictation and 60-second audio capture made it in — a technician wearing gloves isn't going to type a long note in the middle of a corridor. And that's how technical decisions like the persistence adapter and the department × machine cross-validation came about: not because "it's good practice", but because the problem demanded it.
